## Kensington neighbourhoods and streets

### South Kensington

Red-brick mansion blocks line Onslow Square, Cranley Gardens and the streets around Gloucester Road. Many have porters, service lifts and strict move-in rules. We've moved dozens of South Ken flats, so we know which buildings need advance notice, which lifts are too small for a three-seater sofa and which rear entrances to use when the front door opens straight onto the pavement.

Basement flats are common here. External stairs, low ceilings and narrow hallways mean careful measurement and planning. We check head height, door widths and the turning space at the bottom of the steps on the survey.

### Holland Park

Large Victorian and Edwardian houses, many with five or six bedrooms, original features and valuable furniture. Streets like Campden Hill, Holland Park Avenue and the roads around Holland Park itself are often gated or have restricted access. We confirm vehicle access and parking with the residents' association or estate office before the day.

These houses have high ceilings, wide staircases and heavy period doors, but the upper floors can still be tight. Four-poster beds, antique wardrobes and grand pianos need measuring, and we do that on the free survey.

### Kensington High Street and Gloucester Road

Flats above shops, converted mansion blocks and modern developments. Rear access is common—service roads, loading bays or back gates leading to communal bin stores. We check the route on the survey and confirm parking or loading permissions with the freeholder.

### Earl's Court borders, Cromwell Road and Exhibition Road

A mix of period conversions, studio flats and larger family homes. The Cromwell Road corridor (A4) runs through the area, so traffic is heavy during the day. We plan move times around congestion and the congestion charge zone. Parking bays on side streets still need suspensions.

### Kensington Palace Gardens and diplomatic properties

Some roads have security gates, diplomatic missions or additional access rules. If you're moving to or from a property with special access requirements, let us know on the survey so we can arrange clearance in advance.

## Parking and access in Kensington

The Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea operates controlled parking zones across W8, covering most residential streets, mansion block forecourts and mews roads. Parking on a single or double yellow line, in a residents' bay or on a red route without a suspension brings a fine and delays the move.

Bay suspensions typically require [CONFIRM: RBKC bay suspension notice period] working days' notice minimum. We arrange suspensions as part of your move, so give us your moving date as early as possible. The suspension covers the bay outside your property for the hours we need; our vehicle parks there legally while we load or unload.

Kensington sits inside the congestion charge zone. If your move falls on a weekday between 7am and 6pm (and on some weekends during events), the charge applies. We factor that into the planning, not the quote.

Portered buildings and mansion blocks often have their own rules: service-lift bookings, restricted move-in hours (weekdays only, or mornings only), and requirements for floor and lift protection.
